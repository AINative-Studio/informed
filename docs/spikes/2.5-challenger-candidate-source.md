# Spike 2.5: Challenger candidate source

**Story:** 2.5 — Spike: challenger candidate source (2 pts, Must, Iteration 1)
**Status:** Done — written recommendation below. No shippable code; this spike does not change story 2.6's scope, only informs the source it will integrate.
**Question:** Evaluate FEC candidate data against state election office data for challenger lists, so the team picks a reliable source for every candidate on the ballot (prd.md "Challenger promises view", story 9.6; ballot lookup, story 2.4).

## Why these 3 states

We need a mix that stresses the two axes that matter for a national product: ballot volume (does the pipeline hold up at scale?) and how a state's own office actually publishes data (does every state even have a machine-readable source?). Picked:

- **Texas** — largest state by House delegation (38 districts), tests coverage and scale, and its Secretary of State runs on a lagging, batch, document-based process (relevant because several of the largest states, e.g. CA, FL, work similarly).
- **Delaware** — smallest/simplest case (1 at-large House seat, 1 Senate seat up in 2026), tests the low end: does the chosen source work cleanly when there's almost no data to parse, and how fast does a small state's office turn around filings?
- **Pennsylvania** — a 2026 swing state with competitive, closely watched House races (17 districts), where voters most need a complete, fresh challenger list and where campaign activity (new entrants, late filers) is highest.

## Research method

Checked OpenFEC's live API (`api.open.fec.gov/v1/candidates/`) directly with `DEMO_KEY` for all three states' 2026 House races, and looked up each state's official election authority site for its current (fall 2026) candidate/ballot publication. Dates below reflect what was live as of 2026-10-15.

## Findings

### FEC (`/v1/candidates` and `/v1/candidates/search`)

- **What it covers:** Every individual who has filed FEC Form 2 (Statement of Candidacy) for US House, Senate, or President. Each record carries `party`, `state`, `district`, `incumbent_challenge` (I/C/O), `candidate_status`, `has_raised_funds`, `first_file_date`, `last_file_date`, `load_date`.
- **The real coverage gap:** A person only has to register with the FEC — and so only appears in this data — once they raise or spend more than $5,000 in contributions/expenditures (or file Form 2 voluntarily; "testing the waters" activity below $5,000 doesn't trigger it). Verified in the live data that `has_raised_funds: false` records do exist (e.g. PA candidates `ARRIAGA, JESSICA` and `AYOUAZ, ISMAINE` both show `has_raised_funds: false` but are present — they filed Form 2 even without reaching the fundraising bar), so filing status, not fundraising alone, is the actual gate. But a candidate who never files Form 2 — common for minor-party nominees selected by party convention rather than primary, independents who qualify late by petition, and write-ins — never appears, at any freshness.
  - FEC's own Candidate Master File documentation states explicitly that it reflects registered candidates, not who ultimately appears on the ballot.
- **Freshness:** Electronic filings post to the API roughly every 15 minutes; FEC's own `load_date` field on each record confirms this (e.g. a PA candidate who filed 2026-07-06 has `load_date: 2026-07-07T21:17:22` — same business day to next-day). This is materially faster than either state office we checked.
- **Live volume (2026 House candidates, verified via API):**
  - TX: 353 candidate records
  - PA: 107 candidate records
  - DE: 6 candidate records (at-large seat)
- **Access:** Free, keyless for light use (`DEMO_KEY`), or a free api.data.gov key for the full ~1,000 req/hr rate limit. JSON REST API — easy to integrate, well-typed, versioned.

### State election offices

| State | Source | Format | Coverage | Freshness / cadence |

| **Texas** | [SOS Ballot Certification Report](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/forms/2026-ballot-cert.pdf) ([independent filings list](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/elections/laws/independent-declarations-2026.shtml) separately) | One static PDF, generated per county, 1,395 pages for the full state; independents listed as a separate HTML table | Complete and authoritative — every certified candidate (major party nominee + Libertarian + Green + independent) for every office, by county. This is the ballot of record. | Single snapshot, signed/dated 2026-08-28 (general election certification, ~9 weeks before the Nov 3 election, after the March primary and the independent-filing deadline). Not continuously updated; a new PDF is issued only when the Secretary of State re-certifies (e.g. after a ballot change or court order). News coverage from 2026 (San Antonio Report) also flagged technical problems that delayed Texas's candidate-list publication that cycle. |
| **Delaware** | [Dept. of Elections candidate list](https://elections.delaware.gov/candidates/candidatelist/) (per-election HTML page, e.g. `genl_wcddt_2026.html` for write-ins; a parallel page for all filed/qualified candidates) + downloadable Excel export | HTML table per election, plus an Excel download | Comprehensive for the election named — covers write-ins as a distinct, explicitly labeled list in addition to the main qualified-candidate list, which is unusual generosity among the states we found. | Live, rolling updates. The write-in page we fetched carried a visible "Last Updated: 2026-10-15" timestamp matching today, with an explicit disclaimer: "The list is periodically updated. Filings received on a day may not be reported until the next business day." This is the fastest-turnaround state source of the three. |
| **Pennsylvania** | PA Dept. of State, Voting & Elections / "Running for Office" pages (campaign-finance candidate database is a separate system at `campaignfinanceonline.pa.gov`, covering money not the ballot; the actual ballot-qualified list is transmitted by the Secretary of the Commonwealth to the 67 county boards per the published election calendar) | No single public machine-readable file found; distributed to counties as the system of record, with press/Ballotpedia/BallotReady compiling secondary lists from county data | Legally complete once transmitted, but not published by the state as one consolidated dataset we could fetch directly — the authoritative record is at the county level | Per PA's 2026 General Election Calendar, the Secretary of the Commonwealth transmits the full certified candidate list to counties on a fixed date (2026-08-25, about 10 weeks before the Nov 3 election) following filing deadlines of 2026-03-10 (major party) and 2026-08-03 (minor party/unaffiliated). No near-real-time public feed; PA's campaign-finance search tool is live/continuous but only covers state and legislative filers, not a definitive federal ballot list. |

Common pattern across all three states: the authoritative, complete-including-minor-party-and-independent list only exists as a **single late snapshot**, produced after that state's independent/minor-party filing deadline (which is typically mid-to-late summer, well after FEC Form 2 filings for well-funded candidates begin). Delaware is the exception — it maintains a continuously updated public page rather than one certification event, likely because its ballot is small enough to manage that way.

## Comparison

| | FEC `/candidates` | State election office |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage — major party, well-funded | Complete, usually earliest to show | Complete, but only in the final certified list |
| Coverage — minor party / independent / write-in | Only if they clear $5,000 raised/spent and file Form 2; many never will | Complete — this is literally the ballot of record |
| Coverage — late-declared challengers | Appears as soon as Form 2 is filed, often months before any state deadline | Appears only after that state's filing deadline and certification |
| Freshness | ~15 min to next-day via API `load_date` | TX: one certification ~9 weeks pre-election, not continuously updated. DE: rolling, next-business-day. PA: one transmittal ~10 weeks pre-election, no public feed |
| Format | JSON REST API, typed, paginated | TX: PDF. DE: HTML + Excel. PA: no consolidated public file (county-level system of record) |
| Access cost | Free, keyless light use or free api.data.gov key | Free, no key required anywhere we checked, but scraping/parsing cost varies a lot by format |

## Recommendation

**Use the FEC `/candidates` and `/candidates/search` API as the primary source for federal House and Senate candidacies, refreshed on the weekly cadence story 2.6 already specifies, and cross-check against each state's official candidate/ballot list once per cycle after that state's independent/minor-party filing deadline has passed** (concretely: re-pull and reconcile against the state source 1–2 weeks after the latest relevant state deadline, and again at/after ballot certification). Rationale:

1. FEC data is far ahead on freshness (near real-time vs. a single state snapshot weeks before the election) and gives us a normalized, nationwide JSON schema — critical since `JurisdictionAdapter` (prd.md) needs one consistent shape across 50 states rather than 50 bespoke scrapers.
2. FEC data alone is not sufficient for "every candidate on the ballot" (story 9.6, 2.4's own stated goal), because the $5,000-filing threshold and Form-2-only requirement systematically exclude minor-party, independent, and write-in candidates who are nonetheless on the ballot. All three states confirmed this gap exists in practice (e.g. TX's separate independent-filings list, DE's separate write-in list — both outside the normal FEC-shaped flow).
3. State sources are the ballot of record and must be the final word on who appears on a voter's ballot, but they are unusable as the *primary* feed: TX ships one 1,395-page PDF per cycle, PA has no single public file at all (county-level system of record), and even DE's live page explicitly warns of next-business-day lag. None of the three support an automated weekly pull the way FEC's API does.
4. Practical pipeline for 2.6: ingest FEC weekly as the base candidacy list (covers nearly all competitive races and all well-funded challengers early); add a **state-office reconciliation pass** timed to each state's filing deadline (store per-state deadlines, since they vary — e.g. PA's unaffiliated deadline is in August) to pick up independents/minor-party/write-ins FEC will never carry; treat the state certification as authoritative for ballot order and final inclusion, with FEC fields (fundraising, committee links) enriching those records where a `candidate_id` match exists.
5. Because state formats are this heterogeneous (PDF/HTML/Excel/no-file), reconciliation should go behind the same `JurisdictionAdapter` pattern already planned for jurisdictions in the PRD, with a per-state parser — this is non-trivial engineering effort that 2.6 (8 pts) and any later story should budget for explicitly rather than treating "load candidacies" as FEC-only.
